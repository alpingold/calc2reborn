# MA 16200 Study App 2.0: Design Spec

Date: 2026-11-10
Status: design approved in conversation, pending written review

## 1. Purpose

Version 1 is a good quiz: it serves past-exam questions and flashcards and
tracks mastery per lesson. It rarely teaches. After a wrong answer the
student sees "Not quite", the right letter and a lesson link, and 849 of
994 questions have no explanation. The student also has to decide alone
what to do each day and gets little back from the data the app collects.

Version 2.0 turns the quiz into a tutor and fixes the defects found in the
2026-11-10 code review. It stays a static site with no backend.

The owner uses it now for the lessons after Exam 3 and for the final. The
lasting goal is a tool future MA 16200 students can rely on.

### Success criteria

- Every question shows a worked solution after it is answered.
- The app can name the specific problem type a student keeps missing, and
  offer another question of that type.
- Each lesson page walks the student through two or three guided problems.
- The Dashboard tells the student what to do today.
- A student preparing for an exam can see which problem types in its
  lessons are untested or weak.
- Past exam and mock results can be reopened.
- An exam in progress survives closing the tab.
- None of the eight review defects in section 4 remains.

### Assumptions

- Future students arrive by a shared link with no setup, mostly on phones.
- The owner is the only person maintaining content.
- Running costs stay near zero.

## 2. Scope

In 2.0:

- Foundations: the review defects, loading, a linter, the `TakeExam`
  refactor.
- Learning loop: a solution for every question, problem-type tracking, a
  new feedback panel, the Today plan, a readiness panel, attempt history,
  new-card ordering, and guided "Check your understanding" problems.

Not in 2.0, each to get its own spec later:

- Sync across devices and sign-in (planned as 2.1, with phone install and
  offline use).
- A pipeline that delivers students' problem reports to the owner (2.2).
- Full written lessons.
- Partial-credit scoring and free-response questions.
- Trend charts, practice-session history, and preset lesson ranges for
  Exam 1, 2, 3 and the final.
- Typed math answers.
- A projected exam score. With this little data per student it would be
  false precision; the readiness panel shows a real timed mock score.

## 3. Content model

### 3.1 Problem types

Each lesson gains `problemTypes`: two to five entries, each with

- `id`: stable, of the form `<lessonId>-<slug>`, such as
  `L24-ratio-factorial`;
- `name`: a short label shown to students, such as "Ratio test with
  factorials".

Each of the four extra topics gets exactly one type. Review lessons get
none. The full list is about 120 types.

### 3.2 Questions

- `typeIds`: one or two problem-type ids. Each must belong to a lesson
  listed in the question's own `lessonIds`.
- `solution`: required once the content pass is complete (see 3.6).
- `tags`: removed. No question uses it.

### 3.3 Cards

- `typeIds`: zero or more problem-type ids from the card's own lesson,
  naming the types the card supports.
- The existing free-form `tags` stay. Nothing reads them.

### 3.4 Official exam links

`answersUrl` and `solutionsUrl` already exist on official exams and are
not shown anywhere. The feedback panel starts linking them.

### 3.5 Guided problems

New files `content/guided/<lessonId>.json`, one per planned lesson, each a
list of guided problems:

```
GuidedProblem
  id          stable, such as "L24-g1"
  lessonId
  typeId      one of the lesson's problem types
  stem        markdown
  figures?    paths under public/figures
  steps       3 to 5 of GuidedStep
  takeaway    one line of markdown shown at the end

GuidedStep
  prompt      markdown
  choices     2 to 4, labelled consecutively from A
              each: label, text, and note (why it is wrong;
              required on every choice except the answer)
  answer      one of the choice labels
  explanation markdown shown once the step is answered correctly
```

Rules for the content:

- Two or three problems per planned lesson, about 90 in all. Extra topics
  and review lessons get none.
- A lesson's problems cover its most common problem types.
- They are original and easier than exam questions: the aim is the basic
  workflow. None may reuse an exam question.
- Wrong choices are the classic mistakes for that step.

### 3.6 Validation

Added to the existing content test suite:

- Type ids are unique, and each has the form of its lesson's id plus a
  slug.
- Every planned lesson has two to five types; every extra topic has one.
- A question's `typeIds`, where present, belong to one of its lessons.
- A card's `typeIds` belong to its own lesson.
- No question has two choices with identical text.
- Guided problems: three to five steps each; each step's answer is one of
  its choices; every other choice has a note; the problem's type belongs
  to its lesson.

Three rules start lenient and become strict when the content pass
finishes (phase 6):

- every question has a `solution` and at least one `typeId`;
- every planned lesson has at least two guided problems;
- every type has at least three questions. A type with fewer is merged
  into a neighbouring type.

Until then `solution` and `typeIds` are optional in the schema, and a
question without them shows no type features. This lets code ship before
content is complete.

## 4. Foundations

### 4.1 Defect fixes

| # | Defect | Fix |
|---|--------|-----|
| 1 | A mock exam on a narrow range returns no questions after one or two mocks, because the last two mocks' questions are excluded with no fallback (`src/engine/mock.ts`) | When the pool holds fewer than the requested count, top it up from the excluded questions, least recently seen first. The mock form says "up to twelve questions". |
| 2 | An exam in progress is lost when the tab closes; a feedback-mode exam logs nothing until Submit | The session in progress moves from `sessionStorage` to `localStorage`. In feedback mode each answer is logged at Check under the existing stable id `<sessionId>:<questionId>`, so Submit overwrites it. |
| 3 | After reveal, `aria-label` replaces a choice button's text for screen readers (`QuestionView.tsx`) | Remove the `aria-label`. Put "Correct answer" or "Your answer" in visually hidden text inside the button. |
| 4 | Reset and Import leave an exam in progress behind | Both clear the session in progress. |
| 5 | `f2024-e1-q09` (B = C) and `f2017-final-q15` (B = D) have identical choices | Correct both against the source PDFs. The rule in 3.6 prevents a repeat. |
| 6 | The Dashboard says "No cards due today" while new cards are waiting, and counts cards Review skips | Replaced by the Today plan (5.5), which counts due and new cards with the function Review uses. |
| 7 | A card rated Again may not return in the session, since its next due time can fall just outside the one-hour requeue window | A card rated Again always returns later in the same session. A card rated Hard still returns only if due within the hour. |
| 8 | No error screen; an imported `examDate` or timestamp is not validated; results for an exam submitted with no answers vanish on reload | A root `errorElement` with a reload link. Imports validate `examDate` as `YYYY-MM-DD` and timestamps as ISO date-times. The session records in 5.7 fix the vanishing results. |

Notes on defect 2:

- A timed exam's clock keeps running on wall-clock time while the tab is
  closed. Reopening it after time is up shows the existing "ran out of
  time, resuming submits it" notice.
- If a feedback-mode exam is discarded, the answers already logged at
  Check stay in the attempt history. They were real answers. No session
  record is written for it, so it does not appear under Past attempts.
- Two tabs sharing one session in progress overwrite each other, last
  write wins. This is accepted.

### 4.2 Loading

- Content moves out of the main JavaScript chunk into its own chunk,
  loaded by a dynamic import during the existing Loading state, in
  parallel with creating the scheduler. The database opens once content
  is in, because its upgrade (5.7) needs content to rebuild history.
- Screens still receive the whole `Content` object synchronously from
  context. No screen changes.
- Per-exam loading, suggested in `docs/gaps-and-plan.md` item 5.1, is not
  done: mastery and question selection need every question on every
  screen.
- Schema validation of content no longer runs in production on page load.
  It runs in development and in the test suite, which gates deploys.

### 4.3 Tooling

- ESLint with the TypeScript and react-hooks rule sets, run as
  `pnpm lint` and added to `site:deploy` before the tests.
- Each existing `eslint-disable` comment is checked against the real
  warning it suppresses and kept or removed.
- No CI service. The deploy script runs lint, typecheck and tests.

### 4.4 `TakeExam` refactor

A `useExamSession` hook in `src/ui/useExamSession.ts` takes over the
session state, its persistence, the clock, and submission with its frozen
attempts. `TakeExam.tsx` keeps only the rendering for its statuses.

This lands first with behaviour unchanged, using the existing `TakeExam`
tests as the safety net. The storage change in defect 2 follows as a
separate step.

## 5. Learning loop

### 5.1 Problem-type mastery

`lessonMastery` is generalised into one function that scores any grouping
of questions. Problem types use the same rule as lessons: no score until
three attempts, then a recency-weighted average with the existing
constants, weak below 70%. Lesson mastery and the mastery grid are
unchanged.

A question with two types counts toward both.

### 5.2 Feedback panel

After a question is answered, the panel shows, in this order:

1. Correct or Not quite, and the existing notices for derived and written
   answers.
2. The solution.
3. Links to the official answer key and solutions PDF, where the exam has
   them.
4. The problem type by name, and the lesson link.
5. After a wrong answer, the flashcards mapped to the question's types,
   collapsed, readable in place as on the lesson page.
6. After a wrong answer, a link to a guided problem of the same type, if
   one exists.
7. A "Try a similar question" button (5.3).
8. Report a problem.

Items 4 to 7 need `typeIds` and are left out for a question without them.

### 5.3 Similar questions and Practice scopes

Practice accepts two new address parameters alongside `lesson`, and
`from`/`to`:

- `types=<id>[,<id>...]`: questions of these problem types only.
- `retry=1`: only questions whose last answer was wrong at least a day
  ago, within the scope lessons, at most five.

"Try a similar question":

- In Practice, the next question served is another of the same type that
  has not been served this session, preferring one never answered. The
  session then continues as before.
- From exam results, the button opens Practice with `types` set to the
  question's types.
- The button is hidden when no other question of the type is available.

### 5.4 Cards after a miss, and new-card order

The rule that makes every started card in a missed question's lessons due
at once is removed. In its place, the started cards whose `typeIds`
include one of the missed question's types come due now. A miss on a
question without `typeIds` moves no cards.

New cards are introduced in this order, replacing L1 upward:

1. cards for types missed in the last seven days;
2. cards for lessons in the exam range, while an exam scope is active;
3. cards for the current lesson, then each earlier lesson in turn.

Within a group, cards keep their file order. Lessons after the current
lesson still introduce no cards.

### 5.5 Today plan

A pure function `todayPlan` takes the current time, attempts, card states,
guided results, settings and content, and returns up to four items for
the Dashboard. Each opens an existing screen already configured.

| Item | Shown when | Opens | Done when |
|------|-----------|-------|-----------|
| Work through Lesson N | the current lesson has unfinished guided problems | the lesson page, at the guided section | all of them are finished |
| Review n due and m new cards | any card is due or new today | Review | none remain |
| Retry k missed questions | a question's last answer was wrong at least a day ago; k is at most five | Practice with `retry=1` | none qualify |
| Practise your weakest types | always, once any type is in scope | Practice with `types` set to the two weakest types and ten questions | ten practice answers have been logged today |

Scope is the exam lessons while an exam scope is active, otherwise the
lessons up to the current one.

The two weakest types are chosen in this order: types scored below 70%,
lowest first; then untested types in the current lesson; then other
untested types in scope, in course order.

Completion is computed from today's attempts, card states and guided
results, by local calendar day. Nothing new is stored. An item with
nothing to do is left out, and when every item is done the plan reads
"Done for today".

The Dashboard's due-card line and its two action buttons are replaced by
the plan. The onboarding form, the mastery grid and the navigation stay.

### 5.6 Readiness

While an exam scope is active, the Dashboard's countdown box becomes a
readiness panel:

- the countdown line, as now;
- each lesson in the exam range, with its problem types as tiles coloured
  like the mastery grid (untested, weak, ok, strong);
- a count of types tested out of types in range;
- the latest timed mock score on that range, with a button to take one.

When an exam date is set without lessons, the countdown line alone is
shown. The "Weakest lessons" list is removed; the type tiles replace it.

The lesson page gains the same breakdown for its own types, each with a
"Practise this type" link.

### 5.7 History

A new `sessions` table holds one record for each finished exam or mock:

```
SessionRecord
  sessionId
  kind         'exam' | 'mock'
  examId?      for exams
  from?, to?   lesson range, for mocks
  questionIds  in the order shown
  startedAt, finishedAt
  feedback     true if taken with feedback
  correct, total
```

- The record is written at Submit, including when nothing was answered.
- The Exams page gains a "Past attempts" list, newest first, each linking
  to its results page.
- The results page rebuilds a session from its record and attempts. The
  list of mock questions kept in `localStorage` is removed.
- On the database upgrade, records are built from existing attempts
  grouped by session id. An exam's question list comes from the exam; a
  mock's is the questions answered. The same function runs when an older
  export without `sessions` is imported.

### 5.8 Check your understanding

On each lesson page, between Key ideas and Flashcards, a "Check your
understanding" section lists the lesson's guided problems, each marked
once finished. The section carries the label used for extra practice
exams: written for this app, not from Purdue.

Working a problem:

- The statement shows first, then one step at a time.
- A right choice shows the step's explanation and reveals the next step.
- A wrong choice shows that choice's note, disables it, and lets the
  student choose again. The answer is not revealed.
- Finished steps stay on screen, so the page builds into a worked
  solution, and the takeaway appears at the end.
- The app records whether each step was right on the first try.

A new `GuidedProblem` component renders this. It reuses the Markdown
renderer and the choice-button styling, and does not reuse
`QuestionView`, whose reveal-once behaviour differs.

A new `guided` table holds one record for each finished problem:

```
GuidedResult
  problemId
  completedAt
  steps            number of steps
  firstTryCorrect  number of steps right on the first try
```

Finishing a problem again replaces its record. Guided problems do not
count toward mastery, which stays based on exam questions.

## 6. Storage

- Database version 3 adds `sessions` (keyed by `sessionId`, indexed by
  `finishedAt`) and `guided` (keyed by `problemId`). The upgrade backfills
  `sessions` as described in 5.7. Existing tables are untouched.
- `ProgressStore` gains `saveSessionRecord`, `listSessionRecords`,
  `saveGuidedResult` and `listGuidedResults`. Both the IndexedDB and
  in-memory stores implement them.
- Exports gain optional `sessions` and `guided` fields and stay at
  version 1, as `flags` did. Import, reset and clear cover the new tables.
- Attempts are unchanged. Problem-type mastery is computed from existing
  history as soon as questions carry `typeIds`.
- The session in progress lives in `localStorage` (defect 2).

## 7. Content production

1. **Taxonomy.** The problem types for all lessons and extra topics are
   drafted from each lesson's key ideas and its exam questions. The owner
   reviews the full list as one document before anything is tagged.
2. **Guides.** Two new guides, `docs/solution-writing-guide.md` and
   `docs/guided-problem-guide.md`, in the manner of the existing
   transcription and card guides.
3. **Pilot.** One 12-question exam is drafted twice, by Haiku and by
   Sonnet. The drafting model is chosen on the checker's rejection rate
   and the owner's read of both sets. The cheaper model is used unless
   its rework cost makes it the more expensive choice.
4. **Solutions and tags.** One drafting subagent per exam receives the
   taxonomy, the guide, and each question with its keyed answer, and
   writes the solution and `typeIds`. The 145 existing solutions are kept
   and only tagged.
5. **Checking.** A separate subagent on the stronger model solves each
   question without seeing the draft or the key, then compares against
   both. Each question ends as pass, fix, or escalate to the owner. An
   answer that disagrees with the key is reported as a possible
   transcription or key error, so this pass audits the whole bank.
6. **Cards and guided problems.** One subagent per lesson maps cards to
   types and writes the guided problems. The checker verifies every step
   and that no guided problem repeats an exam question.
7. **Owner review.** The development-only `/dev/content` page shows each
   solution and type beside the source page image. The owner sees every
   escalation and a sample of the rest.

Content merges exam by exam and lesson by lesson. When all of it is in,
the three lenient rules in 3.6 become strict.

## 8. Testing

- Tests are written first, as in the existing codebase.
- Unit tests for each new pure function: grouped mastery, `todayPlan`,
  mock top-up, new-card order, cards after a miss, the retry scope, and
  the session backfill.
- The shared storage contract tests cover `sessions` and `guided` for
  both stores. A separate test covers the upgrade from version 2 with
  backfill.
- Component tests for `GuidedProblem`, the feedback panel, the Today
  plan, the readiness panel and Past attempts.
- The existing `TakeExam` tests pass unchanged through the refactor.
- A browser check at phone and desktop widths at the end of each phase.

## 9. Order of work

Each phase leaves the app usable and can be deployed on its own. The
content track waits on the owner's review twice, so it starts first and
runs beside the code.

1. **Foundations.** Section 4. The taxonomy (section 7, step 1) is
   drafted and reviewed during this phase.
2. **Schema for problem types.** Sections 3.1 to 3.4 and 3.6. The guide
   and the pilot (section 7, steps 2 and 3) happen here, and bulk
   solutions follow as soon as the model is chosen.
3. **History, type mastery, feedback panel, Practice scopes.** Sections
   5.1 to 5.3 and 5.7, with the storage changes they need.
4. **Today plan, readiness, card rules.** Sections 5.4 to 5.6.
5. **Guided problems.** Sections 3.5 and 5.8.
6. **Content complete.** The bulk pass finishes and validation becomes
   strict.

## 10. Risks

- **Solution quality from a cheaper model.** A solution can reach the
  keyed letter by wrong steps. The blind check on a stronger model and the
  pilot are the guard. If the pilot shows a high rejection rate for both
  models, drafting moves to the stronger model and the cost is revisited
  before the bulk pass.
- **Taxonomy churn.** Changing type ids after tagging means retagging.
  The owner's review comes before any tagging, and the three-question rule
  is applied at the end by merging, which only rewrites ids.
- **Bundle growth.** About 850 solutions and 90 guided problems add to
  the content chunk. It is separate from the code and cached on its own;
  its size is measured at the end of phase 6.
